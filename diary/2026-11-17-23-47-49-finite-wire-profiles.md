# Finite wire support and narrow profile validation

Recorded: 2026-11-17T23:47:49-08:00 (America/Los_Angeles).

The perspective study left the companion largely missing even with exact
phase geometry. Projection of its clean increment shows why a spatially
uniform representation is a poor fit. Adding the true footprint raises
the eight-knot energy coverage from 17% to 91%. At the exact footprint,
removing the nominal delivery gate raises ISO400 same-plane recovery from
1.84% to 52.29%. Uniform gate removal raises quiet error nearly sixfold.
One signed amplitude per direction and color with exact wire shapes
reaches 84.98% at ISO400 and 34.35% at ISO6400 under the fixed scales.

A compact RAW-reserved phase and width search initially admitted a narrow
shape for both directions on every forced-guide flat and nearly doubled
peak false correction. The margin-one comparison removed those changes
on the retained flats. Fresh noise repeats still exposed a straight-rail
cost when both directions switched to the compact profiles.

The subsequent comparison confined the narrow companion to the prior
reserve-validated footprint of the first active direction and used a
positive reserve margin inside that footprint. Same-plane ISO400 weak
recovery rose from 1.15% to 6.86%, with increment RMS falling from 6.013
to 5.717 DN16. The gain was concentrated in two cases. Fresh forced-guide
controls had similar straight and complex rail error and lower flat RMS.
The archived whole-image guide panels had mixed results. Exact-center
ISO400 main recovery rose from 9.01% to 12.05%, while the corresponding
whole-image guide fell from 4.80% to 4.24%.
