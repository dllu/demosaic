# Close the original V86/V87 context experiment

Both original 4,000-update endpoints and all ten frozen FP32 quality stages
finished. Their training, review, numerical-closure, reference and asset owners
have exited. The independently checked boundary retains the original budgets,
matched source/supervision observations and final RNG states; neither an interim
checkpoint nor a longer budget replaces an endpoint.

Actually viewed all eight final boards at original resolution before opening
numerical quality results: 125 native cells representing 81 source triplets.
Foliage, suspension cords, building wires, weak carriers and neon-wave detail
remain soft. Both new arms reduce selected parent magenta mottling, without a
clear context advantage. The ISO100 GFX image remains a different-aperture,
different-time qualitative reference, not exact GT.

Context minus control photographic PSNR is +0.0009922 dB, with source-bootstrap
95% interval [-0.0011444, +0.0045482]. Known-camera promotion and both weak-carrier
gain criteria fail; all 192 ADC detail-loss limits pass. Neither arm is promoted
or extended. V43 remains the working reference. Canonical camera-linear adverse
values, all conditions and procedural groups remain in the published registry.

Published the complete report, results, separate actual-viewing evidence and
publication checks, with 20 unchanged native PNG assets. Each arm has two exact
synthetic-GT crop triplets and four GFX triplets, including foliage and fence.
Verified report/evidence hashes, native noisy/reference pixel equality, source
and display metadata, and documentation links. README and PROMPT remain unchanged.

sources, whereas native320 requires a 640px bin4 short side and retains 54,059.
The current320 cohort exactly matches its declared 50,912-source filter. These
are geometry counts, not a new training or quality result. With no demonstrated
larger-context photographic benefit, retain256 for the next experiment and
investigate weak-detail recovery. SOTA, broad native superiority and100MP/10s
